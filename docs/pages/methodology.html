Methodology

Where the numbers come from

Every projected figure on this site is derived from a model — not from verified municipal data unless explicitly labeled as such. This page explains how that model works, what its inputs are, and what the numbers do and do not mean.

Important: Projected figures (participation rates, jobs created, dollars circulated, budget deltas) are scenario estimates, not guarantees or verified outcomes. They are designed to illustrate the direction and scale of change the MDF model could produce, not to predict exact results for any specific community.

What “modeled” means

The MDF Compare tool and the Millbrook scenario use a set of adjustable levers — percentage inputs that represent how community participation changes specific categories of municipal spending and local economic activity. When verified checkbook data is not available for a city, these levers are applied to population-scaled baseline estimates.

Think of it as a sensitivity model: it asks, “If community participation changed this lever by X%, what would the downstream effect be?” The model does not simulate human behavior — it applies directional assumptions grounded in comparable real-world programs and economic research.

The model levers

These are the variables the MDF model adjusts. Each represents a category where community self-management is expected to change outcomes relative to a government-dependent baseline.

Variable What it represents How it’s applied Basis for default value
local_multiplier_pct Local money circulation How much longer dollars stay in the local economy before leaking out. 1.0 = no change from baseline. USDA & IMPLAN local multiplier research; community-owned businesses typically retain 2–4× more locally than chains.
revenue_uplift_pct Self-generated revenue increase % increase in community-generated revenue (fundraising, local business growth, fund returns). Observed in participatory budgeting pilots; communities with active economic stewardship show 5–15% local revenue growth over 3 years.
safety_spend_reduction_pct Public safety budget offset Reduction in public safety expenditure driven by community programs that address root causes (food insecurity, youth engagement, mental health support). Research on violence interruption and mutual aid programs; Oakland Ceasefire and similar initiatives showed 10–30% incident reductions.
hs_spend_reduction_pct Health & human services offset Reduction in government health and human services spending when community programs cover basic needs directly. Community health worker programs; mutual aid food systems have documented 10–25% reduction in emergency services utilization.
capital_deferral_pct Capital outlay deferral % of capital projects that can be deferred or reduced when community maintenance programs extend asset life. Preventive maintenance research; community stewardship of public spaces reduces capital replacement frequency.
debt_reduction_pct Debt service reduction Reduction in debt service burden when participatory budgeting reduces reliance on bonds for community needs. Illustrative assumption; participatory budgeting reduces project scope drift and cost overruns.
participation_rate_pct Active community engagement % of residents actively engaged as MDF stewards (volunteers, role-holders, fund participants). Drives all other levers. Comparable community organizing efforts; sustained campaigns achieve 10–25% active participation in mature programs.
community_jobs_created Net new local jobs New employment generated by MDF projects (Community Kitchen staff, coordinators, local business growth). Scaled from MDF role requirements and comparable mutual aid program employment data.
dollars_circulated_locally Dollars retained locally Total dollars that stay in the local economy vs. leaking to outside vendors or chains. Applied via local_multiplier_pct to fund disbursements and local business spending patterns.
vacant_properties_reduced Property activation Number of vacant properties brought into productive community use through housing and business programs. Community land trust and vacant lot activation research; illustrative estimate based on program scale.

The three scenarios

The MDF Compare tool models three scenarios for each city to show a range of outcomes rather than a single projection.

Conservative

Low participation (5–8%), minimal lever adjustments. Represents early-stage organizing with limited community buy-in. Used as the realistic floor.

Moderate

Mid-range participation (12–18%), moderate lever values drawn from comparable real-world programs. The primary reference scenario.

Full Participation

High participation (20–30%), levers at their research-supported upper bounds. Represents a mature, fully-functioning MDF community. Used as the directional ceiling — not a near-term prediction.

About Millbrook: Millbrook is a fictitious city of 35,000 used in the How It Works scenario. Its numbers — crisis response times, participation rates, residents fed, businesses affected — are illustrative projections built from the model levers above, applied to a population-scaled baseline. They represent what the model predicts, not a simulation of real events or a historical record.

When verified data is used

For cities where official checkbook data has been loaded (municipal budget exports, state treasurer records, or audited financials), the model estimates are replaced with actual revenue and expenditure figures. The MDF levers are then applied on top of those verified baselines, not a population estimate.

Data sources by state:

  • Maine: opencheckbook.maine.gov — CSV download, annual
  • Ohio: Ohio Checkbook — CSV download, semi-annual
  • Massachusetts: MA Division of Local Services — CSV download, annual

If verified data has not yet been loaded for a city, the Compare tool displays an amber “Model Estimates Active” banner and provides a link to submit verified data.

What the model does not do

  • It does not simulate individual human behavior or predict how any specific community will respond.
  • It does not account for political resistance, legal barriers, or leadership failures.
  • It does not guarantee that lever values from one community will transfer to another.
  • It does not replace community-specific research, needs assessments, or organizing strategy.

The model is a lens for understanding direction and scale — not a guarantee of outcome. The community determines the actual result.
