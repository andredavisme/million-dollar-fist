Getting Started

One person. One community. Start here.

Before you can rally a community, you have to understand what MDF asks of you — and what it leaves entirely up to you. These two things are very different.

What MDF defines

The framework for what a healthy, self-managing community looks like

MDF establishes the model: community-controlled funds directed to food, housing, health, education, hygiene, business development, and emergency relief. It defines obligations and fund allocation. This part is not up for interpretation.

What your community decides

How that management is built, organized, and sustained in your place

Who runs the meetings. How neighbors are reached. Which problems to tackle first. What organizing looks like in a high-rise versus a rural county. This is yours to answer. MDF is not a global how-to — it is a destination with no prescribed route.

"This blueprint is a starting point, not a script. Every community is different — these tools help you begin the conversation. What you build from there belongs to your community."

Your First 30 Days

  1. Learn the model.

    Read How It Works and sit with it. You don’t need to understand every detail — just be able to explain the core idea to a neighbor in two minutes.

  2. Map your community.

    Use the Community Asset Inventory to identify who and what is already there. Before you ask for anything, find out what your community has to offer.

  3. Have three conversations.

    Pick three people you trust. Tell them what Million Dollar Fist is. Listen more than you talk. What they ask and what they push back on will tell you where to start.

  4. Host a first meeting.

    Use the Event Planning Checklist and the Meeting Agenda Template. Keep it small. The goal is not to launch — it is to show that a real conversation is possible.

  5. Build your list.

    Start a mailing list or outreach journal from day one. Every name matters. The community fund model runs on participation, and participation starts with knowing who’s in.
